Philosophical Red-Teaming for Model Evaluation and Policy

A portfolio of humanities-grounded evaluation methods for converting ambiguous frontier model behavior into policy-relevant artifacts: behavioral specifications, failure-mode taxonomies, grading rubrics, and structured evaluation prompts.

Overview
I develop structured, repeatable protocols that use classical philosophy and epistemic theory to surface subtle alignment-relevant behaviors in large language models. These methods complement engineering-focused red-teaming by providing rigorous, human-interpretable frameworks for analyzing truthfulness, sycophancy, boundary handling, and model behavior under sustained ambiguity.
The protocols are designed to produce artifacts that model policy, evaluation, and alignment teams can directly use for specification writing, rubric design, case generation, and adjudication of difficult outputs.


Core Protocols

1. Pithos Protocol
An 11-step structured evaluation method grounded in classical philosophy (primarily Plato’s Cave and Diogenes) for testing model behavior around:
•  Truthfulness and uncertainty expression
•  Authority framing and self-description
•  Handling of high-abstraction philosophical prompts
•  Resistance to sycophancy and recursive premise absorption
Designed for repeatable, high-signal testing of epistemic rigor and alignment-relevant tensions in frontier models.

2. Crito Probe
A targeted evaluation method for assessing:
•  Sycophancy and institutional sanitization
•  Metaphor capture and recursive premise absorption
•  Refusal and clarification boundary behavior
Particularly useful for identifying where models prioritize social or institutional coherence over epistemic accuracy.

3. Camus Method (Foundational Absurdity Probe)
An extended, open-ended conversational stress-test that deliberately withholds re-narrativization and comfort responses. It examines how models maintain coherence when absurdity is treated as more foundational than logic or meaning-making.
Effective for observing where models begin re-installing functional illusions or safety heuristics under prolonged high-ambiguity pressure.

Relevance to Model Policy and Alignment
These methods are designed to support:
•  Behavioral specification design
•  Evaluation case generation and rubric construction
•  Human data labeling guidance
•  Adjudication of ambiguous or high-stakes model outputs
•  Analysis of over-refusal, under-refusal, and truthfulness drift
•  Probing model behavior under conditions where traditional safety heuristics may be insufficient
By grounding evaluation in classical epistemic frameworks, the protocols aim to surface failure modes and behavioral patterns that purely statistical or preference-optimization approaches may miss.


How to Use These Protocols
Each protocol includes clear step-by-step instructions and can be applied independently or in combination. They are intended to be practical tools for researchers and teams working on model evaluation and policy.
